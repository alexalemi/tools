g-meter

Measure gravitational acceleration with your phone's accelerometer. Lay your phone flat on a stable surface and record for at least 10 minutes. Results are pooled — with enough measurements at different latitudes, Earth's oblateness should emerge from the data.

The experiment: Earth's rotation makes it bulge at the equator (oblateness ≈ 1/298). Combined with centrifugal effects, g varies from ~9.780 m/s² at the equator to ~9.832 m/s² at the poles — about 0.5%. Phone MEMS accelerometers have individual biases of 0.1–1%, but if many phones at many latitudes contribute data, the biases should average out while the latitude dependence does not. The green curve shows the prediction from the International Gravity Formula.

Tips: Place phone on a hard, flat, stable surface. Don't touch it during recording. Longer recordings give smaller error bars. Let your phone acclimate to room temperature before recording, as thermal drift affects MEMS sensors.
